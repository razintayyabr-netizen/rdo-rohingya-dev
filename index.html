Who We Are

Rohingya Development Organisation

RDO is a community-based, non-profit organisation founded in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh. We work with humility, transparency, and deep respect for our community to restore learning, safety, and hope for displaced children and youth.

Our Vision

To build a safer, educated, skilled, and hopeful future for Rohingya children and young people, enabling them to realize their potential and contribute constructively to society.

Our Mission

To support vulnerable Rohingya children and youth through supplementary education, digital literacy, life skills, child protection awareness, and positive youth activities that foster resilience and dignity.

Complementary Role

Supports and complements camp education facilities.

Strict Safeguarding

100% personnel certified in PSEA and child protection.

Gender Parity

≥50% targeted participation for adolescent girls.

Accountability

Continuous MEAL monitoring and community feedback.

Flagship Initiative

RDO Learning Centres for Children & Youth

Four safe, inclusive community learning centres established in Camps 4, 5, 17, and 18 in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh, serving 200 Rohingya children and adolescent youth.

200 Direct Learners

50 Students per Camp Centre

Learners are organized into competency-based groups of about 25 students. Alternating morning and afternoon shifts allow close attention from facilitators and high quality learning.

Structured Schedule

08:30 – 15:00 Daily Operations

Balanced timetable incorporating foundational literacy and numeracy, spoken English, digital workstations, life skills, GBV prevention sessions, team sports, and healing arts.

Community-Led Model

Grassroots Sustainability

Operated by trained community educators with caregiver participation, youth leadership committees, and coordinated referrals to established humanitarian service pathways.

How Each Learning Centre Operates

Designed for maximum child safety, cultural alignment, community ownership, and humanitarian standards.

Phase 1

Service Mapping

Full coordination with camp authorities and humanitarian actors before starting to avoid duplication.

Phase 2

Safe Physical Space

Approved community spaces equipped with study desks, learning materials, and clean facilities.

Phase 3

Trained Educators

Two facilitators per centre (at least one female) trained in child safeguarding and PSEA standards.

Phase 4

Survivor Referrals

Confidential complaints channel with mapped referral pathways to qualified health and protection actors.

Holistic Curriculum

The Six Core Pillars of Development

Every enrolled student receives a balanced mix of foundational learning, technology skills, leadership, and emotional well-being.

1. Foundational Learning

Literacy, Numeracy & English Clubs

  • Basic literacy and numeracy reinforcement
  • Spoken English communication and study habits
  • Reading clubs, peer homework support, and learning games
  • Periodic competency assessments to track individual growth

2. Digital Literacy

Computer Fundamentals & Online Safety

  • Computer fundamentals, keyboard typing, and file management
  • Basic document drafting and digital exploration
  • Online safety, scam protection, and digital hygiene
  • Supervised responsible technology access for displaced youth

3. Life Skills & Leadership

Adolescent Agency & Problem Solving

  • Interpersonal communication, active listening, and teamwork
  • Constructive problem solving and decision-making skills
  • Youth leadership development and civic responsibility
  • Basic household budgeting and future career awareness

4. Child Protection & Rights

Safeguarding & Safe Help-Seeking

  • Awareness of children's rights adapted to displacement
  • Safe disclosure pathways and seeking help when in danger
  • Prevention of child marriage, exploitation, and child labor
  • Child-friendly complaints and feedback channels

5. Gender Equality & GBV

Mutual Respect & Safe Boundaries

  • Dignity, body safety, and mutual respect education
  • Peaceful conflict resolution and healthy interpersonal values
  • Separate sessions for adolescent girls and boys where appropriate
  • Regular dialogue and consultation with parents and community elders

6. Sports, Arts & Recreation

Psychosocial Play & Cultural Healing

  • Organized football, cricket, and collaborative playground games
  • Drawing, painting, and visual storytelling workshops
  • Rohingya folklore, poetry, and cultural expression
  • Trauma-informed recreational activities that rebuild childhood joy
Daily Routine

Centre Daily Schedule & Methodology

Operating Monday through Saturday from 08:30 to 15:00, structured to deliver structured education without causing mental fatigue.

08:30 – 10:00 Shift 1 / 2

Foundational Literacy & Numeracy

Structured math exercises, reading drills, comprehension, and vocabulary development.

10:00 – 11:15 Interactive

English Communication & Reading Clubs

Conversational English, storytelling, peer reading groups, and pronunciation practice.

11:15 – 12:30 Digital Lab

Digital Literacy & Supervised Tech

Computer hardware orientation, keyboard typing, document formatting, and cyber safety.

12:30 – 13:15 Midday

Midday Break & Cohort Transition

Nutritional pause, informal socialization, sanitation refresh, and shift handover.

13:15 – 14:15 Life Skills

Life Skills, GBV Awareness & Protection

Respectful relationships, safe boundaries, personal hygiene, and youth leadership.

14:15 – 15:00 Recreation

Sports, Visual Arts & Psychosocial Play

Team sports, drawing, cultural music, storytelling circles, and confidence building.

Core Operational Priorities

  • Frontline Educators: Qualified community teachers, continuous pedagogy training, and fair stipends.
  • Safe Physical Spaces: Well-ventilated, weather-resilient shelters with clean WASH and child-friendly furniture.
  • Learning Materials: Books, notebooks, writing supplies, and age-appropriate educational games.
  • Digital Workstations: Supervised computers for typing, digital literacy, and basic file management.
  • Safeguarding Oversight: Confidential complaint channels, female protection focal points, and survivor referral pathways.

Risk Mitigation & Safety First

Weather contingencies (cyclones/monsoons) are managed via flexible scheduling and reinforced shelters. Camp approvals and security protocols are mapped in full collaboration with local authorities prior to launch.

Survivor-Centred Protocols strictly enforced across all centres.
Protection & Safeguarding

Strict Child Safeguarding & PSEA Guarantee

The safety and dignity of every child is the bedrock of RDO. All personnel, teachers, and volunteers must undergo vetting and sign our mandatory Child Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct before working with children.

Zero Tolerance for Exploitation & Abuse (PSEA)

Strict adherence to UN Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) standards with thorough background screening.

Confidential Complaints Channels

Child-friendly and caregiver-accessible feedback boxes in every centre with direct female focal point review.

Survivor-Centred Mapped Referral Pathways

RDO does not provide clinical or unauthorized case handling; sensitive disclosures are referred immediately to certified humanitarian actors.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about RDO's community learning centres.

How do RDO Learning Centres coordinate with camp authorities?

RDO operates with full respect for existing humanitarian coordination structures. Before any centre begins operations, site assessments and service mappings are reviewed with relevant camp authorities, RRRC, and education/protection sector coordinators to ensure zero duplication and full safety compliance.

Who are the primary students attending these centres?

Our centres serve 200 Rohingya children and adolescent youth in Camps 4, 5, 17, and 18. Participation is 100% voluntary and free of charge, with a proactive commitment that at least 50% of enrolled students are adolescent girls.

How is child safeguarding and PSEA managed?

Every staff member and volunteer signs our Child Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct. We maintain child-friendly confidential feedback boxes in all centres and have a designated female Protection Focal Point. RDO adheres strictly to survivor-centred referral protocols to qualified clinical and protection actors.

How can an organization get in touch to partner or provide technical assistance?
