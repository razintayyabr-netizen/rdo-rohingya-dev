The Living Reality

Why Community-Based Learning Centres Matter

Over 900,000 Rohingya refugees reside in the camps of Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh. Over half of this population are children and adolescent youth. Living in temporary hillside shelters, they face severe constraints: limited physical space, prolonged school closures, risks of child labor, and heightened protection concerns.

Adolescent girls and young women face heightened vulnerability to early marriage and gender-based violence (GBV), while young boys face violence and severe pressure. RDO's centres offer safe, trusted environments run by qualified community facilitators who speak their language, understand their culture, and nurture their ambitions.

Protection from Exploitation

Safe, monitored spaces that keep vulnerable children off the streets and protected from early marriage.

Continuous Education

Reinforcing basic numeracy, reading, and English to prepare youth for a self-reliant future.

About Our Organisation

Rooted in Community. Dedicated to Future Generations.

The Rohingya Development Organisation (RDO) was founded to directly address the educational gap and safety vulnerabilities faced by young people living in prolonged refugee displacement in Cox's Bazar. We believe that true change comes from empowering community educators, involving caregivers, and building safe spaces where children can regain their confidence.

Our Vision

To build a safer, educated, skilled, and hopeful future for Rohingya children and young people, enabling them to thrive beyond hardship.

Our Mission

To provide accessible supplementary education, basic digital tools, child rights awareness, and psychosocial activities that unlock each child's innate potential.

Coordination Commitment: RDO operates in close consultation with camp authorities, RRRC, and established humanitarian coordination mechanisms to complement—not replace—formal education and protection services.

Daily Learning Centre Model

Operating from 08:30 to 15:00, each centre utilizes two alternating cohorts to optimize space, equipment, and facilitator attention.

10:00 – 11:30

Spoken English, conversational practice, storytelling circles, and reading clubs.

11:30 – 13:00

13:00 – 15:00

Child rights, GBV prevention, body boundaries, football, cricket, and creative arts.

Child Protection & Safeguarding

Our Zero Tolerance Policy & Protection Standards

The safety, dignity, and confidentiality of every learner are the cornerstone of RDO. All teachers, facilitators, and volunteers are screened and must complete mandatory training on Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA).

PSEA & Mandatory Code of Conduct: Strict zero-tolerance protocols aligned with IASC humanitarian standards.
Child-Friendly Complaints Mechanism: Confidential feedback channels reviewed directly by a dedicated female Protection Focal Point.
Survivor-Centred Referral Pathways: Disclosures are handled with immediate care and referred to qualified medical and GBV case actors.

Governance & Staffing Structure

Project Manager & Education Coordinator: Oversees educational pedagogy, community consultation, and humanitarian coordination.
8 Centre Facilitators (2 per Centre): Local community educators delivering daily lessons with gender balance.
Protection & GBV Focal Point: Manages safe disclosure, complaints verification, and coordination with sector leads.
MEAL & Administration Assistant: Maintains attendance records, assessments, and asset management.
